# Sources & References

## Primary Historical Sources

### Aztec Codices
- **Florentine Codex** (Codex Florentinus) — Created by Bernardino de Sahagún, 1575-1577. The most comprehensive documentation of Aztec culture, natural history, and mythology, including descriptions of the ahuizotl.
- **Codex Telleriano-Remensis** — 16th-century Aztec pictographic manuscript
- **Codex Magliabechiano** — Colonial-era codex documenting Aztec religious practices
- Various other Aztec pictographic manuscripts from the pre-Columbian and early colonial periods

### Spanish Colonial Sources
- Sahagún, Bernardino de (1575-1577). *Historia General de las Cosas de Nueva España* (General History of the Things of New Spain / Florentine Codex)
- Durán, Diego (1581). *Historia de las Indias de Nueva España*
- Torquemada, Juan de (1615). *Monarquía Indiana*
- Other Spanish chroniclers' ethnographic accounts of Aztec culture

### Cultural Sensitivity

**Respectful Engagement**:
- Recognize ahuizotl as part of serious religious cosmology, not entertainment
- Respect Aztec cultural heritage and descendant communities
- Consult academic and cultural sources for accurate understanding
- Support preservation of Mesoamerican cultural heritage

### Historical Context

The ahuizotl represents:
- Aztec understanding of natural dangers
- Religious explanation for drowning deaths
- Connection to Tlaloc worship and water deity reverence
- Cultural mechanism for teaching water safety

## Disclaimer

This documentation represents historical and cultural information about the ahuizotl as documented in Aztec sources and scholarly research. The ahuizotl is a mythological creature from Aztec religious tradition, not a cryptozoological target. No modern sightings are claimed or expected.

For authoritative information on Aztec culture and mythology, consult academic sources, museums, and cultural heritage organizations specializing in Mesoamerican studies.
